# Module 8 — Classical Quant

This is where the Python turns into trading. The patterns in this module — factor models, momentum and mean reversion, pairs trading with rolling Kalman betas, portfolio construction with Kelly and CVaR — are the bedrock the rest of the course's strategies sit on. They are not glamorous. They are the strategies that actually print money at real funds, decade after decade.

Pages:

1. **[Factor models — building blocks for everything](01-factor-models.md)** — the cross-sectional return decomposition; Fama-French in practice; how to think about an "alpha" against a factor model.
2. **[Momentum and mean reversion](02-momentum-meanrev.md)** — the two oldest signals in the book, why they persist, and how to size them.
3. **[Ornstein-Uhlenbeck and stationary spreads](03-ornstein-uhlenbeck.md)** — the workhorse mean-reverting process; OU calibration; trading the spread.
4. **[Cointegration and pairs trading](04-cointegration.md)** — Engle-Granger, Johansen, and a full pairs strategy with a Kalman-filtered hedge ratio.
5. **[Portfolio construction: Markowitz, Black-Litterman, risk parity](05-portfolio.md)** — mean-variance and its problems; the Black-Litterman view-augmented prior; risk parity and HRP.
6. **[Sizing — Kelly, fractional Kelly, CVaR with cvxpy](06-sizing.md)** — the optimal-growth fraction; why nobody uses full Kelly; CVaR-constrained sizing.
